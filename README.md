The analysis examined unemployment trends across Indian states using monthly unemployment data. The primary objective was to understand the effects of COVID-19 on unemployment, identify regional and seasonal patterns, and generate insights that could inform labour market and economic policies.
The results show that unemployment increased substantially during the COVID-19 period, demonstrating the severe disruption caused by nationwide lockdowns and restrictions. There were also considerable differences among states, suggesting that the pandemic affected regions unevenly depending on their economic structures and labour market conditions.
Detailed Findings
1. Data Quality Assessment

The dataset originally contained 768 observations, but 28 records contained missing values across all variables. These incomplete records were removed, resulting in 740 complete observations used for the analysis. Duplicate records were also checked and removed, ensuring the dataset was suitable for reliable analysis.

Interpretation

The cleaning process improved the reliability of the analysis by ensuring that statistical summaries and visualizations were based only on complete records.

2. Overall Unemployment Characteristics

The descriptive statistics reveal substantial variation in unemployment across states and time.

Key observations include:

Mean unemployment rate: 11.79%
Median unemployment rate: 8.35%
Maximum unemployment rate: 76.74%
Minimum unemployment rate: 0%

The large difference between the average and maximum unemployment rates indicates that some states experienced extremely severe unemployment shocks during the pandemic.

Interpretation

This variability suggests that COVID-19 did not affect all regions equally. States with greater dependence on informal employment, tourism, manufacturing, or service industries were likely more vulnerable to lockdown-related job losses.

3. State-Level Unemployment Patterns

The analysis of average unemployment rates by state highlighted significant regional disparities.

The report identified:

Tripura as the state with the highest average unemployment rate.
Meghalaya as the state with the lowest average unemployment rate.

Interpretation

These differences indicate that labour market resilience varied considerably across states. Factors such as industrial diversification, government support programmes, labour force composition, and economic development likely contributed to these disparities.

This finding suggests that national employment policies alone may not adequately address unemployment, and state-specific interventions may be required.

4. Monthly Unemployment Trend

The monthly trend analysis showed noticeable fluctuations in unemployment throughout the observation period.

The time-series visualization demonstrates that unemployment remained relatively stable before COVID-19 but increased sharply after the onset of the pandemic.

Interpretation

The sudden rise coincides with nationwide lockdown measures implemented during early 2020. Business closures, travel restrictions, reduced consumer demand, and interruptions to economic activities likely contributed to widespread job losses.

The gradual decline observed after the initial spike may indicate the reopening of businesses and gradual economic recovery.

5. Impact of COVID-19

One of the most important findings is the comparison between unemployment before and during COVID-19.

The average unemployment rate increased from:

Before COVID: 9.51%
During COVID: 17.77%

This represents an increase of approximately 87%, indicating that unemployment nearly doubled during the pandemic.

Interpretation

This result provides strong evidence that COVID-19 had a significant negative effect on employment opportunities across India.

The increase reflects:

Business shutdowns
Reduced production
Supply chain disruptions
Reduced consumer spending
Restrictions on movement
Temporary closure of many industries
6. Employment Trend

The employment trend analysis showed an inverse relationship between employment levels and unemployment rates.

During periods when unemployment increased, the number of employed individuals declined.

Interpretation

This inverse relationship confirms expected labour market behaviour: as businesses reduced operations during COVID-19, fewer people remained employed.

7. Labour Force Participation

The labour participation trend showed moderate fluctuations over time.

Unlike unemployment, labour force participation did not fluctuate as dramatically.

Interpretation

This suggests that although many people remained willing to work, employment opportunities were limited due to the economic slowdown rather than a lack of labour market participation.

8. Correlation Analysis

The correlation matrix revealed important relationships among the variables.

The analysis concluded that:

Employment has a negative correlation with unemployment.
Labour participation exhibits only a weak relationship with the other variables.

Interpretation

This indicates that:

As employment increases, unemployment decreases.
Labour force participation alone does not strongly predict unemployment because many people continued seeking employment even when jobs were unavailable.
9. Seasonal Patterns

Monthly averages revealed seasonal differences in unemployment.

The highest average unemployment occurred during April, which aligns with the strictest COVID-19 lockdown period.

Interpretation

The spike reflects the immediate impact of business closures and movement restrictions.

Later months show declining unemployment, suggesting gradual economic recovery as restrictions eased.

Overall Conclusion

The analysis clearly demonstrates that the COVID-19 pandemic had a profound impact on employment in India.

The findings indicate that:

Unemployment increased significantly during the pandemic.
Regional disparities existed, with some states experiencing much higher unemployment than others.
Employment declined sharply during lockdown periods.
Labour force participation remained relatively stable despite rising unemployment.
Seasonal analysis identified April as the period of greatest unemployment pressure.
Correlation analysis confirmed the expected inverse relationship between employment and unemployment.

Overall, the results suggest that India's labour market was highly sensitive to economic disruptions caused by the pandemic.

Recommendations

Based on the findings, the following recommendations are proposed:

1. Implement Targeted Employment Programmes

States with persistently high unemployment—particularly those identified as having the highest average rates—should receive targeted employment interventions, including public works programmes, wage subsidies, and job creation initiatives.

2. Strengthen Social Protection Systems

Governments should expand unemployment benefits, food assistance, and income support during economic crises to reduce financial hardship for unemployed workers.

3. Invest in Workforce Reskilling

Training programmes focused on digital skills, information technology, healthcare, renewable energy, and other growing industries can help displaced workers transition into emerging employment opportunities.

4. Support Small and Medium-Sized Enterprises (SMEs)

SMEs are major employers and should receive financial assistance, tax incentives, and access to affordable credit during periods of economic uncertainty to preserve jobs.

5. Improve Labour Market Monitoring

Real-time unemployment monitoring systems should be strengthened to enable policymakers to respond quickly to sudden increases in unemployment.

6. Promote Regional Economic Development

Because unemployment varies significantly across states, policies should be tailored to regional economic conditions rather than adopting a uniform national approach.

7. Build Economic Resilience

Governments should develop contingency plans for future economic shocks by diversifying industries, strengthening supply chains, and encouraging sectors that are less vulnerable to disruptions.

8. Encourage Public–Private Partnerships

Collaboration between government agencies, educational institutions, and private employers can expand internship opportunities, apprenticeships, and employment pathways for young people.
